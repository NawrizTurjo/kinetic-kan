\section*{\sffamily\color{accent}Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

This report documents a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network Neural ODE (KAN-ODE)
\cite{koenig2024kanode,liu2024kan,chen2018neuralode} project that
reproduces the base paper's Lotka-Volterra benchmark, extends it to two
additional dynamical systems (a damped pendulum and an SIR epidemic model),
diagnoses and fixes two genuine cross-domain training failures, and runs
five independent follow-on studies probing solver order, differentiation
method, basis-function design, stiffness, and comparison against sparse
symbolic regression.

\begin{keypoint}[Headline results]
\begin{itemize}
\item KAN-ODE's extrapolation error is essentially flat across eight
  limit-cycle periods ($1.1\times$ degradation), while a parameter-matched
  MLP degrades $8.3\times$ --- a $\mathbf{40.6\times}$ KAN-ODE advantage at
  the longest horizon tested, materially stronger than the $5.4\times$
  near-horizon comparison.
\item The pendulum failure ($R^2=-1.318$) was fixed by extending the
  training window; SIR's divergence traced to a $19\times$ unit-scale
  mismatch at initialization, fixable by a change of time units and a
  conservation projection. SIR extrapolation $R^2$ moved from $-20.97$ to
  $+0.9981$.
\item Adjoint sensitivity already uses less memory than direct autograd at
  every trajectory length tested ($17.1$ vs.\ $18.6$--$21.5$~MB), but costs
  $1.9$--$2.1\times$ wall-clock --- direct autograd remains the correct
  default at this project's scale.
\item Gradient-norm roughness does not explain Euler's convergence floor
  (Spearman $\rho=-0.088$, $p=0.87$); shared, order-independent spike bursts
  dominate all six solvers' gradient traces.
\item A learnable spline/RBF basis blend never beats the better pure basis on
  either system tested, and on the pendulum it overfits catastrophically past
  $\sim$3{,}000 epochs in a way pure RBF does not.
\item The stiffness--solver stability analysis traced failures at
  $\mu\in\{0.5,1.0,2.0\}$ to random seed rather than solver order; the
  $\mu=2.0$ trap is a genuine, solver-independent spurious equilibrium
  confirmed from trained weights and from an independent loss-landscape
  analysis.
\item Sparse symbolic regression is far more noise-robust than KAN-ODE
  ($9\times$ vs.\ $1540\times$ degradation, $\sigma=0\to0.1$) but strictly
  less accurate at every level tested; a real, non-mechanistic epidemic curve
  confirmed that two of SIR's three stability fixes transfer by their
  original mechanism.
\end{itemize}
\end{keypoint}

Every number and figure in this report is traceable to a saved training
record or a regenerable figure from this project's own experiment logs.
A validation stage (epoch-budget sensitivity, paper-implementation audit)
is fully designed but has not yet executed; its completion is the natural
next step for extending confidence in the results above.
