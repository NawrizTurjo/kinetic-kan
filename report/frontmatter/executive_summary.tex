\section*{\sffamily\color{accent}Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

This report documents a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network Neural ODE (KAN-ODE)
\cite{koenig2024kanode,liu2024kan,chen2018neuralode} project that
reproduces the base paper's Lotka-Volterra benchmark with every integrator
and basis implemented and verified from its defining formula, analyses the
trained vector fields as dynamical systems, extends the recipe to two
additional dynamical systems (a damped pendulum and an SIR epidemic model),
diagnoses and fixes two genuine cross-domain training failures, runs five
independent follow-on studies, and validates the budget-sensitive
conclusions at up to five times the training budget.

\begin{keypoint}[Headline results]
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Solvers.} All six integrators pass their order conditions to
  round-off and show their design order. Above order two, solver choice does
  not change accuracy; Midpoint matches Tsit5 within $0.3\%$ at $29\%$ of the
  cost. Networks learn the field their solver needs: the Euler model matches
  the inverse modified equation of backward error analysis, and no model
  transfers across solver families.
\item \textbf{What is learned.} Every trained model, KAN or MLP, is an
  attracting limit cycle around a spurious unstable equilibrium, where the
  true system has a neutral centre. The models extrapolate the training orbit
  and say nothing reliable about any other.
\item \textbf{KAN versus MLP.} At $10^4$ epochs KAN-ODE extrapolates
  $5.4\times$ better than a parameter-matched MLP (a $48\times$ smaller
  period error), widening to $40.6\times$ at twice the horizon. At
  $5\times10^4$ epochs this \emph{reverses}: the MLP is $6.8\times$ better.
  The paper's literal tanh MLP never trains, and the paper's tenfold speed
  claim holds only against it.
\item \textbf{Bases.} B-spline and RBF are tied at both budgets; global
  polynomials extrapolate $4$--$5\times$ worse. Locality, not conditioning,
  separates them.
\item \textbf{Cross-domain fixes.} The pendulum failure ($R^2=-1.318$) was
  fixed by extending the training window; SIR's divergence traced to a
  $19\times$ unit-scale mismatch at initialization, fixable by a change of
  time units and a conservation projection. SIR extrapolation $R^2$ moved
  from $-20.97$ to $+0.9981$.
\item \textbf{Follow-on studies.} Adjoint sensitivity uses less memory than
  direct autograd ($17.1$ vs.\ $18.6$--$21.5$~MB) at a $1.9$--$2.1\times$
  wall-clock cost; gradient-norm roughness does not explain Euler's floor
  ($\rho=-0.088$); a learnable spline/RBF blend never beats the better pure
  basis; stiffness-map failures trace to the random seed; sparse symbolic
  regression is far more noise-robust than KAN-ODE ($9\times$ vs.\
  $1540\times$) but less accurate at every noise level tested.
\end{itemize}
\end{keypoint}

Every number and figure in this report is traceable to a saved training
record, a checkpoint re-scored by the report's own analysis script, or a
regenerable figure. All results are single-seed; multi-seed replication of
the KAN-versus-MLP crossover is the most valuable next step.
