# Project Title: Stochastic-Deterministic Synthesis: From Runge-Kutta to Metropolis-Hastings in Bayesian Epidemic Inference

### 1. The Core Concept (The "Posh" Factor)
While other groups will likely pick a trendy AI paper (like KANs or SLAM) and blindly call `loss.backward()` in PyTorch—completely bypassing the actual numerical math—your group will build a **complete, from-scratch numerical and simulation pipeline**. 

You will tackle the classic 1978 English Boarding School Influenza outbreak (a famous, tiny, real-world dataset). You will model the epidemic using Ordinary Differential Equations (SIR model), solve them using custom-built Runge-Kutta solvers, and then use a hand-coded Metropolis-Hastings Monte Carlo algorithm to infer the disease parameters ($\beta, \gamma, R_0$) with full Bayesian uncertainty quantification. 

**The God-Tier Insight that will win you the highest marks:** 
You will demonstrate the profound numerical link between ODE solvers and Monte Carlo simulation. Specifically, you will show that using a low-order ODE solver (Euler) introduces numerical truncation errors that make the likelihood landscape rugged, causing the Metropolis-Hastings sampler to fail (poor mixing, low acceptance rates). You will then prove that transitioning to a 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) solver smooths the likelihood surface, allowing the MCMC to efficiently converge. *This explicitly proves to the professor that you understand why numerical analysis matters in simulation.*

To stand out, you cannot just reproduce a paper; you must extend it. Here is your 5-person execution plan:

**Phase 2: The Stochastic Simulation Core (Students 3 & 4)**
*   **Task:** Hand-code the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm. 
*   **The Likelihood:** Do not use a naive Gaussian likelihood. Use a **Poisson likelihood** (since daily case counts are discrete events). The expected value $\lambda_t$ is generated by your RK4 solver. 
*   **Execution:** Run the MCMC to generate 10,000 posterior samples for $\beta, \gamma$, and $R_0 = \beta/\gamma$. Tune the proposal distribution to achieve the theoretical ~23-44% acceptance rate.

**Phase 3: The "Punchline" & Extension (Student 5 - The Analyst)**

*   **The Structural Extension:** The 1978 dataset is known in literature to be poorly fit by a standard SIR model because the influenza had a latent period. Implement the **SEIR model** (adds an Exposed compartment). Extend your RK4 solver to 4D. Re-run the MCMC. Show how $R_0$ shifts dramatically (from $\approx 3.5$ to $\approx 10$). 
*   **Cross-Domain:** Apply your entire pipeline to a completely different dataset—the 1665 Eyam Plague outbreak (a classic historical dataset).
